If Week 1 was a statement, Week 2 was a pixelated battle zone. The Seattle Seahawks traveled to the desert and absolutely dismantled the division-rival Arizona Cardinals, 35 - 21. Even without the sidelined Rashid Shaheed, Seattle’s offense didn't skip a beat, turning the Cardinals' secondary into a collection of stationary sprites.

The ground game was so dominant that Arizona’s defensive line looked like they were stuck in a permanent grappling animation. Meanwhile, Sam Darnold continued his MVP-caliber efficiency, throwing darts with the kind of zigzag accuracy that defies physics while Jadarian Price showed why people are so high on his talent.

When asked after the game if playing in the dry desert heat posed any challenges, Darnold offered a casual shrug. "Honestly, the AC in the stadium was great," Darnold said, adjusting his cap. "Coach MacDonald told us to just keep running straight until the defenders fall over. So we did."

The real fireworks, however, came from the defensive side of the ball. The Hawks' pass rush terrorized the Cardinals all afternoon, making big plays whenever Arizona tried to mount a comeback. The only bright spot was rookie running back Jeremiyah Love, who nearly equaled Price's production for the day. 

Linebacker Ernest Jones, fresh off his Week 1 media fine, was spotted by this reporter in the tunnel. When asked if he had any revised thoughts on the few critics who picked Arizona to upset them, Jones stopped, stared deeply into the camera, and muttered, "They committed the cardinal sin." He then winked. Once again, this publication has heavily edited the transcript to keep our advertisers happy.

Over in the locker room, wide receiver Jake Bobo was seen laughing with the training staff while icing his hands. When asked his opinion on why the game seemed lopsided, he shrugged. 

"The curse is real, man. The Cardinals looked like they were playing with broken controllers out there for a minute. I'm telling you, whatever juju is on that ink Big Cat mentioned last week, it's our not-so-secret weapon. Maybe we'll make some t-shirts out of it."

With a 2-0 start and a point differential that commands respect, Seattle looks utterly unstoppable. If they keep playing like this—and if Ernest Jones doesn't get the team banned from the airwaves—the road to the Super Bowl goes through the Pacific Northwest. Up next, the Washington Commanders.
